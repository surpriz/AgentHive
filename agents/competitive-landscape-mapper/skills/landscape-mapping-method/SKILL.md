# Landscape mapping method

## Axes

Pick axes that (a) matter for the decision in the question and (b) actually separate the
players. Common axes: segment (size, vertical, geography), breadth (point tool, suite),
price model (flat, per seat, per entity, free), delivery (self-serve, with a human, done for
you), core differentiator, direction of travel (upmarket, downmarket, vertical). Two to four
axes make a readable map; six is the limit.

## Cells

Each cell comes from the notes: quote or close paraphrase. Unknown is unknown. A cell filled
from memory contaminates the map and the decision built on it.

## Groups

Players with similar values on the decisive axes form a group. Name the group by what they
share ("suites", "freelancer tools", "status quo"). A player can belong to one group only; if it
straddles, note it.

## Gaps and crowds

A gap is a combination of values no player occupies. Before calling it an opportunity, write the
caveat: the segment may be too small, the need may not exist, regulation may block it, or the
notes may be incomplete. A crowd is a combination with three or more players: expensive to
enter on the same axes.

## Partners versus competitors

Competitor: overlapping offer in the same segment. Partner candidate: same segment, adjacent
offer (a bank next to a bookkeeping tool), or a channel to the segment (an accountant
marketplace). Say which axis makes the difference.

## Presentation

A markdown table with players as rows and axes as columns, `unknown` written out. A line per
group. A line per gap with its caveat. The decision-relevant reading in three sentences.
