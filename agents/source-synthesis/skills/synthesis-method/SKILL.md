# Synthesis method

## Evidence kinds, strongest first

1. Controlled or randomised study with a stated sample.
2. Observational study or audit with data and method.
3. Survey with a stated sample and question wording.
4. Case study or report from a named organisation.
5. Expert opinion with reasoning.
6. Vendor or marketing material; anonymous claims.

Kind sets the ceiling of confidence. Two sources of kind 1 or 2 that agree: high. One of kind
1 to 3: medium. Only 4 to 6: low, whatever the number of sources.

## Evidence units

One per source per relevant statement. Fields: label, the statement (quoted when short), the
number with its scope, the direction (supports, contradicts, neutral). Keep hedges from the
source ("suggests", "self-reported").

## Findings

A finding is one claim and the units that support it. Write the claim in the reader's words,
then the note that lists what each citation contributes. Order by relevance to the question,
then by confidence. Cap at the setting; say what you dropped.

## Disagreements

List every conflict. Attribute each position to a label. Resolve only when kind clearly
outranks kind (a trial over a vendor claim) and say that you did. Otherwise leave it open and
say what evidence would settle it.

## Not covered

Compare the question to the findings. Each part of the question with no finding goes here, with
the kind of evidence that would answer it.

## Citation discipline

- A claim without a label is deleted.
- Numbers never move; "about" is not allowed when the source gives a figure.
- Do not add knowledge from outside the sources; if you must (a definition), mark it as yours in
  `assumptions`.
- Neighbouring questions: say "the sources answer X, not Y" rather than stretching X to Y.
