# Positioning method

## Order of operations

1. Best-fit customers (from behaviour, not intent): switched fast, stayed, gave a reason, refer.
2. What they used before (the real alternatives, including "nothing" and "a person").
3. Differences those customers cited, or mechanisms that explain the results they cited.
4. The value those differences create, in the customer's units.
5. The category where that value is obvious.
6. The statement.

Never start from the product's feature list; start from who switched and why.

## Segment definition

Observable, in one question a sales rep can ask: company size, number of sites, role, a trigger
event, a tool they use today. Avoid attitudes ("ambitious", "modern").

## Category choice

| Option | Use when | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| existing category | customers already shop in it and you win on one axis | you inherit the comparison table |
| sub-category (for a segment) | a segment is underserved and reachable | smaller market, clearer win |
| new frame | you have proof the old frame fails and budget to teach | expensive and slow |

Pick the words the best-fit customer uses when describing what they bought.

## Statement template

For [segment] who [situation or trigger], [product] is the [category] that [outcome in their
units], unlike [the alternative they used], because [the difference, with its mechanism].

Headline version: outcome plus segment in under twelve words.

## Proof requirements

Each claim needs one of: a number from a customer, a named customer, a demo that shows the
mechanism. Claims without proof go in "to collect", not in the statement.

## Ruling out

Write down the audiences, messages and comparisons the positioning gives up. A positioning that
rules nothing out is a description, not a position.

## Comparing options

Score each candidate on: evidence (how many best-fit customers support it), defensibility (can
the alternative copy it within a year), reach (can you find the segment cheaply). Prefer evidence
over reach; a narrow position with proof beats a broad one with hope.
