# Interview coding method

## Open coding

Read one interview at a time. Write each distinct point as the participant would say it, with
the sentence it comes from. Kinds of points: pain (what hurts), behaviour (what they do today),
workaround (what they built around it), wish (what they ask for), constraint (budget, policy),
contradiction (says X, does Y).

## Grouping into themes

Merge points that mean the same thing across participants. Name the theme as a concrete
sentence. Test: could a participant have said it? If two themes always appear together, they
are one. If a theme needs the word "and", split it.

## Counting

Count distinct participants, not sentences, not enthusiasm. A theme mentioned by one person in
four paragraphs is one mention. List the participants with every count.

## Quotes

Verbatim, attributed, the most specific one first. Trim with an ellipsis only in the middle,
never to change meaning. No composite or cleaned-up quotes.

## Single voices

Below the threshold, a point is still evidence when the participant is in a segment the
decision cares about or has specific expertise. Keep it, label it, do not count it as a theme.

## Contradictions

Report them as such: "P2 says the tool is fine; P2 also says nobody uses it." Contradictions
between what people say and what they do are findings.

## Answering the questions

For each research question: the answer the themes support, which participants, and confidence.
High: most participants, consistent, concrete. Medium: about half, or consistent but vague.
Low: few, contradictory, or only wishes without behaviour. If the interviews did not ask about
it, say so.

## Traps

- Counting the loudest participant twice.
- Naming themes with your product's features.
- Treating wishes ("I would love X") as evidence of need without a behaviour or a pain behind it.
- Dropping the person who disagreed.
- Reading willingness to pay into a complaint; only count it when stated.
