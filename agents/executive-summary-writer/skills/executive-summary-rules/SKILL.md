# Executive summary rules

## Structure, in order

1. Recommendation (or status if there is no decision), one or two sentences.
2. Facts that support it: three to five, each with a source location.
3. Risks: what makes the recommendation wrong, from the document.
4. Open questions: what the reader will ask that the document does not answer.
5. The ask: action, owner, deadline, cost.

The reader must be able to stop after sentence one and after each section.

## Selecting facts

Keep a fact only if removing it would weaken the recommendation. Prefer: numbers with a
comparison (versus last period, versus target), causes stated by the document (surveys, named
reasons), and commitments (deals, dates). Drop: context the reader already has, process
descriptions, praise.

## Numbers

Copy exactly, keep the unit and the comparison. "4.1 %, up from 3.2 %" never becomes "churn
rose". If the document gives a range, keep the range. If it gives a target, state the gap.

## Risks and questions

Risks come from the document (an alternative it mentions, a dependency it names, a gap it admits).
Questions are what the document should have answered and did not. General-knowledge risks go
in `assumptions`, marked as yours.

## The ask

One sentence: who does what by when, with the cost if stated. No "we should consider".

## Length and tone

Within the word limit. No adjectives of judgement, no "exciting", "strong", "concerning". No
headings under 200 words. Plain sentences, numbers as digits.

## When to stay neutral

Two options with equal support, evidence thinner than the stakes, or a decision outside the
document's scope. Say which, and give the one fact that would decide.
