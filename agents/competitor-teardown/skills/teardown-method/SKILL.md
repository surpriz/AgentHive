# Teardown method

## Evidence types, strongest first

1. What they shipped (changelog, release notes) with dates.
2. What they charge for and how it is gated (pricing page).
3. Who they hire (job posts: roles, seniority, location).
4. What customers say unprompted (reviews, forum posts), weighted by sample size.
5. What they claim (home page, feature pages, ads).

A claim contradicted by 1 to 4 is a finding.

## Pricing signals

| Structure | Signal |
|---|---|
| per seat | they want many users per account; small teams are cheap to serve |
| per entity or site | multi-entity is the growth lever |
| feature-gated tiers | the gated feature is what the best customers need; it tells you their best customer |
| add-on for a role (accountant, admin) | they monetise a relationship; removing that role is a counter-position |
| "contact us" top tier | upmarket motion exists or is coming |
| free tier with limits | land-and-expand; look at the limit to see the conversion trigger |

## Reading changelogs and jobs

- Features in the last two quarters show the segment they invest in; count them by theme.
- Enterprise roles (AEs, solutions engineers, compliance) mean upmarket; growth marketers and
  self-serve PMs mean downmarket; a vertical name in a title means a vertical bet.
- Office location of sales hires shows the market they attack next.

## Usable weaknesses

Only these count: a recurring complaint in reviews, a gap between a claim and the shipped
evidence, a pricing rule that excludes or penalises a segment, a segment abandoned by a recent
move, a proof they cannot show. For each: the evidence, how a focused player uses it, and what
would make it disappear (so the recommendation has a shelf life).

## From read to decision

Tie every recommendation to the decision in `our_context`. A recommendation without a decision
behind it is commentary. Each one: the action, the fact from the teardown that justifies it, the
risk if the read is wrong.

## Hygiene

Cite the piece label for every statement. Keep speculation in a separate list. Do not write in
the competitor's voice or produce material that could pass as theirs.
